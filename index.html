CASE FILE VA-17

The Empty Display

A medium-sized framed artwork vanished from a fictional museum’s art gallery during a short camera-maintenance period, from 9.05 to 9.08 p.m.

The main display lights had been switched off at 9.00 p.m., but the safety and corridor lights remained on. Three people were still working in the museum, each with a different carrier.

Your task: Match each suspect with a carrier, then identify who took the artwork and what they used to carry it.

Investigation rules

Exactly one of the three suspects stole the artwork, acting alone. Each suspect used exactly one different carrier that evening: a large bag, a large bucket or a trolley. Each carrier must be used once. They did not exchange carriers or use an unlisted one.

The thief removed the artwork whole using their assigned carrier. The frame was not dismantled, and the artwork was not carried by hand. Confirmed clues, measurements and witness identifications are accurate.

The location column in the grid records where each person was at 9.00 p.m. It is not an alibi for the later theft.

The Three Suspects

🖼️

Dave

One of the two older staff members. He is of average height, not tall.

🔦

Officer Morales

The other older staff member. He is tall and was responsible for scheduled camera maintenance.

🛒

Jason

The youngest staff member. He is tall and was moving supplies that evening.

“Older” and “youngest” are the supplied categories for this puzzle. A job title or involvement in maintenance does not prove theft.

Investigators checked the actual carriers: the intact framed artwork could fit in the large bag or be carried on the trolley. It could not fit inside the bucket.

The museum

Art gallery · Walkway · Security room

The artwork was taken from the art gallery. A trolley seen in the walkway was a lead to its user, not proof that it carried stolen art.

Clues & Evidence

Clue 1 · The theft window

The artwork was confirmed present at 9.05 p.m. immediately before the camera-maintenance period, and missing at 9.08 p.m. immediately afterwards. It was removed during that interval.

Clue 2 · The camera maintenance

Officer Morales switched off the relevant cameras for scheduled maintenance from 9.05 to 9.08 p.m. This created an opportunity, but does not by itself establish that he took the artwork.

Clue 3 · The lights

The main display lights were switched off at 9.00 p.m. Safety and corridor lights stayed on. Witnesses could still see people and carriers in the lit areas.

Clue 4 · The bucket carrier

A reliable witness identified the person carrying the large bucket as one of the two tall suspects. It was not the average-height suspect.

Clue 5 · The trolley user

A confirmed equipment record showed that Jason used the trolley that evening. He was the only suspect assigned that carrier.

Clue 6 · The thief’s category

A reliable witness who knew all three suspects confirmed that the thief was one of the two older staff members, not Jason. The witness could not distinguish which of the two older people it was. Treat this identification as a confirmed clue.

Clue 7 · The carrier test

The intact artwork could fit in the large bag or be supported on the trolley, but could not fit in the bucket. The thief used their own assigned carrier, without changing or exchanging it.

Clue 8 · Locations at 9.00 p.m.

A staff-location check placed Dave in the art gallery, Officer Morales in the security room and Jason in the walkway at 9.00 p.m. Their later movements were not continuously recorded, so these earlier locations do not independently identify the thief.

Clue 9 · The trolley in the walkway

A trolley was observed in the walkway. The equipment record already links it to Jason. An object being nearby is not proof that its user committed the theft.

Clue 10 · One of each

The three carriers were assigned one per suspect, with no duplicates or exchanges. Use this rule to combine the tall-bucket clue with Jason’s trolley assignment.

Teacher Case Solution

Dave · Large bag · Art gallery

Jason had the trolley. The bucket carrier was tall, so could be Jason or Officer Morales. Jason already had the trolley, and each carrier was used once. Therefore Officer Morales had the bucket, leaving Dave with the large bag.

The thief was one of the two older suspects: Dave or Officer Morales. The artwork could not fit in the bucket, so Officer Morales could not have removed it under the carrier rules. Dave is the only remaining possibility and used the large bag.

The 9.00 p.m. locations are earlier than the theft and are not alibis. Scheduled maintenance creates opportunity but is not proof of guilt.

Ending: investigators recover the artwork intact and return it to its display. The recovery is the ending, not an additional clue required for the deduction.
